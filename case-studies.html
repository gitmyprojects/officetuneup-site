Built for a previous employer

Contracts built from the sales record, not by hand

The problem: typing the same information into three places

The situation

Customer contracts started from a master Word document. Staff copied it, typed in the customer and job details, and edited the terms as they went. Word's track changes showed some edits, but not all of them were captured, and anyone with access to the master could change it.

Mistakes surfaced only when a customer noticed them.

What changed

A Generate PDF Document button now sits on the sales record. One click pulls the customer, address, job site and project details already entered there and fills them into an approved contract template.

The right contract package is chosen automatically by the state the project is in (Oregon or Washington), and the finished contract arrives as a PDF, ready to send.

Before

  1. Copy the master Word file
  2. Type in customer and job details
  3. Edit the terms by hand
  4. Customer spots the mistake

After

  1. Details entered once in the sales record
  2. Click Generate PDF Document
  3. Correct state's contract filled in
  4. PDF ready to send

What it meant for the team

  • Details are entered once, in the sales record, instead of being retyped into each contract.
  • Every contract starts from the same approved wording. Changing the terms means deliberately updating the template, not editing a copy.
  • Nobody has to remember which state's version to use.

Built with: Dynamics 365 Sales, Power Automate, Word templates

Built for a previous employer

One production board for purchasing and the shop

The problem: finding out about problems too late

The situation

Job status lived in the company's main business system, while the production team tracked actual start and finish dates in a separate list. Seeing where a job stood meant checking both.

When purchasing learned that a material delivery would be late, there was no set place to tell production.

What changed

It started as a verbal request. I designed and built a single board that combines both sources: one line per job, showing whether it's finished, on the floor or not started, with running totals for the year and quarter.

A notes column gives purchasing one place to flag anything that could slow production, such as a late material delivery, right next to the job it affects.

Before

  1. Check job status in the main system
  2. Check dates in the production list
  3. Late materials mentioned in passing

After

  1. One board, one line per job
  2. Purchasing adds a note to the job
  3. Production sees it beside the job

What it meant for the team

  • Purchasing and production management look at the same list instead of two.
  • Possible delays are written down where production will see them, instead of passed along in conversation.
  • Job status can be read at a glance.

Built with: Power BI, SharePoint, the company's existing business system

Built in a test company

Approvals that route themselves

The problem: chasing approvals by email

The situation

In a test company I set up to practice, every change to how the business runs needs sign-off from the person who owns that process. Changes that affect the financials also need a review from the controller.

Done by email, nobody can easily tell who has a request, who approved it, or whether the right people were asked at all.

What changed

Submitting a request now sends it to the right approver automatically, based on their role rather than a name written into the setup. Financial changes go on to the controller.

One person can't approve their own request or approve it twice; if that would happen, it goes to their manager instead. Every decision is recorded with who made it, and the outcome is written back to the request.

Before

  1. Email the request
  2. Wait and follow up
  3. Search the inbox for who approved

After

  1. Submit the request
  2. Sent to the process owner
  3. Financial review if needed
  4. Decision recorded on the request

What it meant for the team

  • Requesters can see where their request stands without asking.
  • When someone changes roles, approvals follow the role. Nothing has to be rebuilt.
  • There's a clear record of every approval if an auditor asks.

Built with: SharePoint, Power Automate, Microsoft 365 groups

Built in a test company

One home for every procedure

The problem: hunting for the latest version of a document

The situation

In the same test company, the scenario came from a quality audit: an operator followed an outdated work instruction because an old copy was still sitting on a shared drive.

Procedures lived in several places, in several versions, with no clear owner.

What changed

Procedures and work instructions now live in one library. Each document must have an owner, a department and a next review date before it can be saved.

Drafts stay hidden while they're being revised. Everyone else only ever sees the current approved version, and every past version is kept along with who approved it.

Before

  1. Copies on several shared drives
  2. Which one is current?
  3. The old version gets used

After

  1. One library for every procedure
  2. Drafts hidden until approved
  3. Everyone sees the current version
  4. Reviewed once a year

What it meant for the team

  • Staff find the current version in one place, every time.
  • Authors can revise a document without confusing anyone.
  • Every document comes up for review once a year instead of going stale.

Built with: SharePoint

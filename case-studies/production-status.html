Real-world project

Purchasing and production aren't on the same page

One production board for purchasing and the shop

The situation

Job status lived in the company's main business system, while the production team tracked actual start and finish dates in a separate list. Seeing where a job stood meant checking both.

When purchasing learned that a material delivery would be late, there was no set place to tell production.

What changed

It started as a verbal request. I designed and built a single board that combines both sources: one line per job, showing whether it's finished, on the floor or not started, with running totals for the year and quarter.

A notes column gives purchasing one place to flag anything that could slow production, such as a late material delivery, right next to the job it affects.

Before

  1. Check job status in the main system
  2. Check dates in the production list
  3. Late materials mentioned in passing

After

  1. One board, one line per job
  2. Purchasing adds a note to the job
  3. Production sees it beside the job

What it meant for the team

  • Purchasing and production management look at the same list instead of two.
  • Possible delays are written down where production will see them, instead of passed along in conversation.
  • Job status can be read at a glance.

Built with: Power BI, SharePoint, the company's existing business system

Real-world project

Fuel tax reporting is a scramble

Trip logs that turn into the fuel tax filing

The situation

The company's trucks ran in Oregon and Washington, and the fuel tax filing (IFTA) requires the miles driven and fuel bought in each state.

Drivers logged every trip, but turning that log into filing numbers, and catching entries that would make the filing wrong, was manual work.

What changed

A report turns the trip log into the numbers for the filing. Pick a date range and states to see total miles and gallons, broken down by vehicle, with every trip listed underneath as the record behind the totals.

A second page flags trips whose odometer readings don't line up. Selecting a flagged trip opens it in an editing panel inside the report, so the person who spots the problem fixes it right there.

Before

  1. Pull the trip log
  2. Total miles by state by hand
  3. Hope the odometer readings match

After

  1. Pick dates and states
  2. Miles and gallons by vehicle
  3. Bad entries flagged and fixed in place

What it meant for the team

  • Filing numbers come straight from the trip log.
  • Errors are caught and corrected before the filing goes in.
  • Every total is backed by the trips behind it.

Built with: Power BI, Power Apps, Dataverse
